Patterns
Learned rules extracted from your historical Klaviyo data. Star patterns you want the calendar and copy generators to prioritize.
How to read these patterns
Confidence badges explained
  • High. 50+ supporting campaigns AND a strong effect (open rate delta 3+ pts, CTR 0.5+ pts, or RPR $0.05+). Act on these.
  • Medium. 20 to 49 campaigns OR 50+ with a smaller effect. Worth acting on but validate first.
  • Exploratory. Under 20 campaigns or weak effect. Treat as a hypothesis, not a conclusion.
  • Calibration note. Badges are computed from sample size and effect size, not self-assessed. This prevents the AI from over-claiming on thin data.
Assessing a pattern
  • Trust the numbers, not the narrative. Read the evidence field. If numbers do not support the rule, dismiss it.
  • Gut-check against brand. You know SFH better than the data. A pattern like "use urgency" might conflict with Ines voice. Dismiss when strategy and stats disagree.
  • Check the example winners. If the 3 sample subject lines do not obviously demonstrate the rule, the pattern is likely overreaching.
  • Watch for seasonal or segment bias. BFCM and highly engaged segments skew results. A pattern that leans on these is narrower than it looks.
  • Specific predicted numbers are often decorative. Claude sometimes writes precise-looking forecasts like "lifts opens from 28% to 38%" that sound authoritative but are educated guesses. The direction is usually right; the magnitude is often fabricated.
  • The pragmatic test. Would you confidently act on this in your next send? Yes, star it. No, dismiss it.
When to re-run extraction
  • Monthly, after new Klaviyo uploads. Fresh data can surface new patterns or strengthen existing ones. Make it part of your SFH reporting rhythm.
  • When switching time ranges. All time shows foundational winners. Last 12 months shows current relevance. Last 90 days catches recent shifts.
  • After a big launch or promo. Major campaigns can shift what is working. Re-extract to see what changed.
  • Once is enough for a given range. Patterns save permanently in Supabase. You only re-run to analyze new data or a new time window.
  • Compare All time vs Last 12 months. Patterns that show up in both are durable. Patterns only in one view are worth watching.
  • Duplicates happen. Running extraction twice on the same range can produce similar patterns. Dismiss the dupes manually for now.

Flow Diagnostics
Live flows by health, with diagnostic findings underneath. Scan the top, drill into a critical flow, fix the worst messages.
How to read this page
The workflow
  1. Scan flow cards. Each flow card shows a Health Score and 8 metrics. Use the score as a quick scan signal to find flows worth investigating.
  2. Pick 2 to 3 flows to optimize. Toggle "Sprint" on the cards or specific messages you plan to work on this month. Sprint-toggled flows pin to the top of the list.
  3. Diagnose a flow. Click "Diagnose this flow" to generate findings. Each diagnose run replaces the prior findings for that flow on the current snapshot, so no duplicates.
  4. Triage findings. Expand the findings block to see specific findings. Click the green checkmark to accept (will appear in reports) or red X to reject (hidden from reports). Findings start as undecided.
  5. Do the work in Klaviyo. Quill recommends; the actual changes happen in your ESP. Re-import the next month to see the impact via month-over-month deltas on each metric.
The Health Score (0 to 100)
  • What it is. A weighted score across 8 metrics (Open, Click, Click-through, Conversion, Revenue per Recipient, Unsubscribe, Spam, Bounce) compared to Klaviyo industry benchmarks for the Health and Wellness vertical.
  • What 100% means. All 8 metrics meeting or exceeding industry averages. Positive metrics (open, click, etc.) score against the industry target; negative metrics (unsub, spam, bounce) score against the deliverability threshold.
  • How to use it. A scan signal, not a verdict. A 92% flow might still have one critical issue. A 58% flow might be working hard but on a tough audience. Always check the 8 underlying metrics before deciding what to optimize.
  • Color bands. The donut ring is colored to give a fast scan signal: red (0 to 49) means below benchmark, orange (50 to 79) means within range with room to grow, green (80 to 100) means above benchmark. The same colors appear on the individual metric tiles below: red = below benchmark, orange = within range, green = above benchmark.
  • Color is not a verdict. Different flow types perform differently. A welcome flow naturally scores higher than a win-back. A 60% (blue) win-back may be excellent for that audience; a 60% welcome flow may be underperforming. Always check the 8 underlying metrics before deciding what to optimize.
  • Benchmarks refresh annually. Klaviyo publishes new industry data each year (typically Q1). The score recalibrates when the benchmarks are updated. Currently sourced from Klaviyo 2025 + 2026 Benchmark Reports.
The 8 metrics
  • Engagement: Open Rate, Click Rate, Click-through Rate. CTR (clicks divided by opens) is sharper than raw click rate because it isolates body performance from subject line performance.
  • Revenue: Conversion Rate, Revenue per Recipient. Conversion is how often a delivery turns into a purchase. RPR is the dollar amount per delivered email.
  • Deliverability: Unsubscribe, Spam, Bounce. All three are negative metrics. Above-threshold values damage list health and inbox placement for your whole sending domain.
  • Month over month deltas. When prior snapshot data exists, each metric shows the change from the prior month. Empty if no prior snapshot.
Findings and the accept/reject workflow
  • Findings come from two layers. The deterministic rule engine catches red-metric thresholds (e.g., unsub over 0.5 percent, spam over 0.1 percent) every time. The LLM adds interpretive findings on top.
  • Findings are collapsed by default. Click the row to expand and review them.
  • Accept (green checkmark) means it will appear in reports. Reject (red X) hides it from reports but keeps it visible in the UI for audit. Click an already-active button to clear back to undecided.
  • Re-diagnosing replaces all findings for the flow. Your accept/reject decisions on the prior batch are not migrated forward. Make sprint-level decisions before re-diagnosing if the prior batch matters.
  • Severity tags. Fix now (act this sprint). Opportunity (test next sprint). Monitor (watch). A/B test (specific hypothesis to validate).
Sprint toggles
  • Flow-level toggle. The big "Sprint" pill on each flow card marks ALL messages in that flow as in-sprint. Useful when you're overhauling a whole flow.
  • Message-level toggle. The smaller "Sprint" pill on each message row toggles that single message. Useful when you only want to fix one specific email.
  • Sprint state persists across months. A flow stays in-sprint until manually toggled off. Designed for multi-month optimization work.
  • Flows with any sprint-marked message pin to the top of the list.
When to re-diagnose
  • After monthly Klaviyo upload. Fresh metrics, fresh findings.
  • After acting on a fix in Klaviyo. Wait for 2 to 3 weeks of new data first; diagnoses on stale data won't reflect the change.
  • Re-running on unchanged data produces similar findings. The system is deterministic on the rules layer and roughly stable on the LLM layer.
Honest limitations
  • Quill does not see subject lines or body copy. The Klaviyo flow CSV export does not include them. Findings about subject or body recommend reviewing them in Klaviyo, not analyzing the text directly.
  • Quill does not see send order or time delays. Message names like "Email 1" and "Email 2" are labels, not authoritative position data. Findings will not claim a message sends in any particular order.
  • The score weights are defaults, not science. 40 percent engagement, 30 percent revenue, 30 percent deliverability. Reasonable for SFH but defensibility comes from the underlying metrics, not the weighted number itself.

Voice Brief
SFH voice and strategy reference. Structured fields drive hard constraints in generators. Narrative sections provide context. Edit anything to keep it current.
Loaded
How the Voice Brief is used
What generators read
  • Structured fields on the left. Voice adjectives, signature phrases, banned words, and banned claim types become hard constraints in every prompt. Generators cannot output banned language.
  • Narrative sections on the right. Brand snapshot, customer profile, differentiation, and positioning give Claude context for the deeper "why" behind SFH output.
  • Editing flows through immediately. Changes save on blur. Next Ideas or Draft run picks up the new voice.
When to update
  • Positioning shifts. If SFH launches a new product line or changes market positioning, update the positioning and differentiation sections.
  • New banned language surfaces. Any time you dismiss generated copy because of a word or phrase, add it to banned words so it never comes back.
  • Signature phrases evolve. When a winning phrase emerges from campaigns, add it here so Claude can reach for it.
Products
SFH product catalog. Ideas and Draft generators read from here to avoid inventing product details or claims. Edit each product to refine its description, ingredients, signature claims, and stack pairs.
How Products ground generation
What generators read
  • Short description. The 1-2 sentence pitch. Used when an email features the product.
  • Key ingredients. Keeps Claude from inventing "clinical doses" or ingredient claims.
  • Signature claims. Brand-approved factual phrases. Claude pulls from this list rather than writing its own.
  • Stack pairs. Guides Claude when suggesting routines. Behavioral framing, not biochemical.
  • Notes. Anything worth Claude knowing about how SFH talks about this product.
Tips
  • Refine signature claims from real product pages. The seeded claims are starting points. Paste what your product page actually says.
  • Stack pairs are behavioral. "Creatine + Magnesium" means they fit in the same routine, not that one enhances the other.
  • Notes is where judgment lives. If a product has a specific talking point you dont want generators missing, put it here.
Ideas
Generate email concepts grounded in SFH voice, your starred patterns, and your starred flow diagnostics. Ideas become the backbone of your calendar and the starting point for drafts.
How Ideas generation works
What Claude sees when generating
  • Selected bucket definition. Name, description, key patterns, hook examples, and "what makes it good" from the Buckets tab.
  • Your starred patterns. Every pattern you starred under Patterns. If you have none, Claude uses your active patterns.
  • Your starred flow diagnostics. Starred flow findings so ideas can address known gaps and losers.
  • Voice brief structured fields. Banned words, banned claim types, signature phrases, POV, formality all become hard constraints.
  • Voice brief narrative sections. Brand snapshot, customer profile, differentiation, positioning as context.
  • Optional extra context. Anything you add in the form (seasonality, launch, campaign focus).
Assessing an idea
  • Check the hook. Would you actually want to open this email? If not, dismiss.
  • Check the grounded-in tags. Every idea cites which starred pattern or diagnostic shaped it. If the attribution feels thin or wrong, the idea is probably off.
  • Star what you want to build. Starred ideas feed Draft generation next.
  • Promote to calendar. Click "Add to Waiting Bay" to schedule the concept for a real send.
If generation feels repetitive
  • Star patterns more aggressively. More starred intel gives Claude more distinct angles to draw from.
  • Use extra context. "Creatine launch in 2 weeks" or "Coming off BFCM, focus on new-year health reset" pushes generation toward fresh territory.
  • Dismiss aggressively. Dismissed ideas arent shown to Claude on next run, so you wont get the same idea twice.

Draft
Generate full email drafts from a starred idea. Ines structure: headline, subheadline, first CTA, body, bridge, product section, final CTA. Two variants per draft for A/B testing.
How Draft generation works
What Claude sees when generating
  • The source idea. Hook, angle, subject options, preview, CTA angle, reasoning. The draft stays faithful to the idea.
  • The bucket definition. Keeps the draft shaped to the bucket format.
  • Voice brief. Structured fields are hard constraints. Narrative fields are context.
  • Top winning subject lines. The best historical subjects from your Klaviyo data serve as voice anchors.
  • Optional extra direction. Anything you add in the form (product focus, specific angle refinement, length tweaks).
Variant strategies
  • Different angle. A and B explore different angles of the same idea. Better for testing which hook framing resonates. More divergent, requires judgment on which lands.
  • Tonal variant. A is the main take. B is the same angle executed with a different tone or emphasis. Better for subject-line A/B testing while keeping the core message consistent.
Working with drafts
  • Pick a preferred variant. Click the A or B radio to mark which you prefer. This feeds future copy generation.
  • Star what is close. Starred drafts surface in the Campaign Editor when we build it next session.
  • Dismiss to regenerate. The idea stays starred. You can generate a fresh draft from the same idea anytime.
  • Add to Waiting Bay creates a calendar row with the preferred variant prefilled.

Gaps analysis

Pre-planning briefing. Run this before planning a month or a week to see what is missing: calendar coverage, bucket mix, starred-but-undrafted ideas, and drafts still waiting to land on the calendar.
How Gaps analysis works
What it is

A pre-planning briefing. Run this before sitting down to plan your month or week. It shows what is missing so you can plan with the gaps in mind, not discover them after.

What the date range means

The date range is your planning window. It is the period you are about to plan or review. Gaps looks at what is currently scheduled on the calendar inside that window and flags what is missing.

If you want to plan May, set the range to May 1 to May 31. Gaps will tell you which weeks of May are empty, which buckets are under-represented, and which starred ideas you have not yet drafted for May.

Target cadence

Campaign cadence is how many sends you want per week (SFH is 1 per week). Gaps uses this to decide what counts as a gap. A week with zero sends when the target is 1 per week is flagged as below cadence. A week with 3 when the target is 1 gets an over note. Set to 0 if you do not want cadence-based flagging.

The value is saved to Supabase, so it persists across devices and reloads. You rarely need to touch it.

Three sections in the output
  • Calendar coverage. Week by week view of what is scheduled in the range. Empty weeks flagged in orange.
  • Bucket mix. Distribution of upcoming emails across the 5 SFH content buckets. Flags for "unused" (no sends), "light" (1 send across a 6+ email range), and "heavy" (one bucket more than half of sends).
  • Workflow queue. Starred ideas not yet drafted, and drafts not yet applied to the calendar. These are active queue items that always show regardless of date window.
When to run
  • Before monthly planning. Set the range to next month, see what is already committed, plan what to fill.
  • Before a weekly review. Set the range to the next 7 to 14 days. Catch empty Fridays before they become empty Fridays.
  • After generating ideas. See whether starred ideas have turned into drafts, and whether drafts have turned into calendar rows.

Re-run any time. The briefing is a point in time snapshot, not a live dashboard.
